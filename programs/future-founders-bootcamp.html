The Gap

What the school system is not teaching your child

Memory, not seeing

IB, IGCSE, CBSE, ICSE — every curriculum rewards the recall of what is already known. None of them teach your child to see what is not yet obvious.

Sameness on applications

Two applicants, same grades, same test scores, same extracurriculars. "I built a venture from scratch in 8 weeks" is a different signal than "I was president of a club."

Leadership as a title, not a craft

Being elected head boy is not the same as leading a team through ambiguity. Your child needs to know how to decide, disagree, and ship — not how to chair a meeting.

The Method

Four dimensions of seeing the world

The bootcamp is built on a single idea: entrepreneurship is a way of seeing the world, and that way of seeing can be taught. We use physics as the lens — because physics is the most rigorous description we have of how things actually move, change, and compound.

D1 · Seeing Clearly

Strip problems to what is true

First Principles · Experimentation · Inversion · Compounding

D2 · Seeing Change

Why some ideas move and others don't

Inertia · Friction · Momentum · Activation Energy · Catalyst

D3 · Seeing Systems

The unit of progress

Quantization · Bottleneck · Flywheel

D4 · Seeing Scale

How ideas stop being local

Critical Mass · Surface Area · Leverage

In Practice

Built, not just memorised

Every framework is applied to your child's own venture. No "case study" detachment — they use the lens to build their own thing, week by week.

Why Physics

The cleanest description of how things move

Physics gives us a vocabulary that is precise, tested, and portable. The same lens works in a school project, a startup, or a life decision.

The Curriculum

8 weeks. 36 hours. One finished venture.

Three sessions per week, each 1.5 hours, all live and cohort-based. The first half is solo: your child picks a problem, talks to humans, designs a solution. The second half is in teams: they lead, they disagree, they ship.

WEEK 1 · SEEING THE PROBLEM

First Principles · Inversion · Idea Hexagon

SESSIONS 1–3

How to see what others miss

Before your child can build, they have to see. We start with the foundational question: how do you know a problem is worth solving? They learn to strip assumptions, invert the question, and generate ideas from multiple directions.

  • First Principles decomposition of a real problem
  • Inversion exercise: how would you guarantee this fails?
  • Idea Hexagon: 30 candidate problem areas

Deliverable: A shortlist of 5 problem areas worth exploring further.

WEEK 2 · TALKING TO HUMANS

Customer Discovery · Question Design · Bias Spotting

SESSIONS 4–6

Listening as a craft

Most teenagers (and most adults) are bad at interviews. This week is practice. Your child designs questions, runs 5 real interviews with people outside their circle, and learns to hear what is actually being said versus what they want to hear.

  • 5 customer interviews (transcribed and synthesised)
  • Bias audit: where did I lead the witness?
  • Pattern map: what themes are emerging?

Deliverable: A synthesised insights document from real conversations.

WEEK 3 · DESIGNING THE SOLUTION

Minimum Viable Belief · Trade-off Mapping · Friction Mapping

SESSIONS 7–9

From problem to solution

Your child has a problem and they have data from real humans. Now they design a solution that is the smallest thing that could actually work — and they map every trade-off they are making so the decisions are conscious, not accidental.

  • Solution concept with explicit assumptions
  • Friction map: what does the user experience today vs. with your solution?
  • Rough prototype or detailed written description

Deliverable: A solution concept and a prototype in some form.

WEEK 4 · WILL IT WORK? WILL IT SCALE?

Bottleneck Analysis · Flywheel Design · Critical Mass

SESSIONS 10–12

The business model and the growth loop

A good idea is not enough. This week your child figures out the unit economics, identifies the bottleneck that would kill the venture, and designs a flywheel — the self-reinforcing loop that makes growth compound.

  • One-page business model (revenue, costs, customers)
  • Bottleneck analysis: the one thing that, if fixed, changes everything
  • Flywheel diagram: how does this thing actually grow?

Deliverable: A one-page business model + an early growth loop.

WEEK 5 · BUILDING THE DECK

Pitch Architecture · Storytelling · Data Visualisation

SESSIONS 13–15

The pitch as an artifact

If you cannot explain it, you do not understand it. Your child builds a real investor-style pitch deck — not a school presentation. The deck is the cumulative artifact of the first 4 weeks, and it is what they will pitch on Demo Day.

  • Full pitch deck (problem, customer, solution, model, traction plan)
  • Story arc: the narrative thread that holds it together
  • Practice pitch + peer critique

Deliverable: A complete pitch deck, first draft.

WEEK 6 · INTO TEAMS

Team Formation · Role Clarity · Decision-Making Under Pressure

SESSIONS 16–18

Leadership begins

The bootcamp pivots. Your child joins a team around a shared project. They define roles (not "leader" — specific responsibilities), establish how decisions will get made, and start shipping under real constraints.

  • Team charter: who decides what, and how
  • Defined roles with explicit accountabilities
  • First team deliverable (a working artifact, not a slide)

Deliverable: A working team, defined roles, and a joint project in progress.

WEEK 7 · LEADING THE PROJECT

Feedback Loops · Disagreement Protocols · Activation Energy

SESSIONS 19–21

The work of leading

The team ships. Your child is responsible for a piece of it. They receive feedback — from peers, from the facilitator, from the work itself. They learn that disagreement is information, and the highest-leverage move is often the smallest one.

  • Team output (whatever the project is producing)
  • Individual leadership reflection: what did I learn about how I lead?
  • Critique received and acted on

Deliverable: A working team output + an individual leadership reflection.

WEEK 8 · PITCH DAY

Live Presentation · Fielding Questions · Receiving Critique

SESSIONS 22–24 · DEMO DAY

🎓 The pitch that ends the bootcamp

Live pitches to a panel of founders, operators, and educators. Your child has rehearsed, received structured feedback, refined, and presented. They walk away with a recording, a written assessment, and a real artifact for their college applications and future opportunities.

  • Live pitch (5 min + 5 min Q&A) to outside panel
  • Written feedback from judges
  • Pitch recording (their property, theirs to use)
  • Written letter of assessment suitable for college applications
  • Certificate of completion + alumni status

Final Deliverable: Live pitch, recorded + assessed.

FAQ

Common questions from parents

My child has never studied physics formally. Will they be lost? +

No. The physics is a lens, not a curriculum. We use concepts — inertia, friction, momentum, activation energy, critical mass — as thinking tools. Your child does not need any physics background, only curiosity.

How is this different from an entrepreneurship summer camp? +

Summer camps tend to be short, broad, and surface-level. The Future Founders Bootcamp is 36 hours of live, cohort-based work over 8 weeks, with a tangible deliverable at the end. Your child is not "exposed to entrepreneurship" — they build a venture.

Is this good for college applications? +

Yes — but indirectly. Your child will leave with a pitch deck, a business model, a leadership reflection, and a written letter of assessment. More importantly, they will have something distinctive to talk about in essays and interviews that 90% of applicants do not have.

What if my child is shy or has never pitched before? +

Most of our students have never pitched. The entire second half of the bootcamp is designed to build them up to pitch day. By week 8, they have rehearsed, received structured feedback, and refined. Shyness is not a barrier — it's the starting point.

How much parent involvement is expected? +

Minimal during the program. We ask parents to (1) ensure the student can attend all sessions, (2) read the mid-program and end-of-program reflections we send, and (3) attend the final pitch day if in Jaipur or join via livestream if not.

What happens after the bootcamp ends? +

Your child retains access to all frameworks, worksheets, and templates. Outstanding students are invited to apply for the Future Founders Fellowship — a 5-month program where they enter an international competition (Blue Ocean Strategy or NASA Space Settlement).
