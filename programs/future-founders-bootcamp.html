What this is

A snapshot of entrepreneurship. Not a certificate about entrepreneurship.

Most teen programs stop at a slide deck. Future Founders Bootcamp does not. In eight weeks we compress the real loop into something a teenager can actually run:

1. Start from the problem

We do not start with “what product should I make?” We start with what is broken for a real person. First principles. Inversion. Conversations with humans outside their circle.

2. Build something

A digital product, a simple website, a D2C thing — whatever the problem demands and whatever can ship in this window. They build it. I do not build it for them.

3. Learn to sell it

Calling. Emailing. DMing. The idea has to leave the room. A pitch to judges is the last week. Selling to humans is the work.

It has the high-energy parts. It has the boring parts. That is the point. They should know what entrepreneurship actually feels like before they decide they want more of it.

The lens

Physics of Entrepreneurship

I do not teach formulas. I teach the laws that already govern how things move — then we use them on the venture. Friction, momentum, bottlenecks, leverage, compounding. Once they see it, they cannot unsee it.

D1 · Seeing Clearly

First Principles · Inversion · Compounding

A named problem that would survive a sceptical adult — not a school-project prompt.

D2 · Seeing Change

Friction · Inertia · Activation Energy

Where people actually hurt, and a solution that can ship in 8 weeks.

D3 · Seeing Systems

Bottleneck · Flywheel

A model that does not collapse when someone asks “how does this make money?”

D4 · Seeing Scale

Leverage · Surface Area

Outreach. A pitch. Proof the idea left the room.

Who this is for

Built for the student who will do the work

A fit if…

  • They are 13+ and restless with “what’s the point of school.”
  • They have ideas — or are annoyed that they don’t — and will put hours in between sessions.
  • They will talk to strangers, get ignored, and ship something imperfect.
  • You want them to know what entrepreneurship is, not add another activity to a list.

Not a fit if…

  • They want a fun activity and a line for the college essay without the work.
  • The family wants us to “handle it” while the student spectates.
  • You need a guaranteed admission, a funded startup, or a revenue number in writing.
  • You want two-week summer-camp energy. This is eight weeks of directed work.
They walk away with

Proof they can build, and proof they tried to sell

A product, not a hypothetical

Something they built from scratch in the window — digital, D2C, or another form the problem demanded.

A business model they can explain

Who it is for, how it makes money, where it bottlenecks. Stress-tested in the room.

An outreach trail

Calls, emails, DMs — logged. Not theoretical. Most programs skip this. We do not.

A live pitch

They present the work. They field questions. That recording is theirs.

What they do not walk away with: a promised revenue number, a funded company, or a promised college letter. Those are not ours to sell. Students who do the work have something real to point to. That is the point.
