The Problem

Most parents want their child to make their own decisions. Almost nobody teaches them how.

Schools teach compliance. Parents want independence. In between, the student is stuck — freezing on choices, outsourcing direction, or drifting into whatever is easiest.

I start at around 12 because that is when this actually lands. For the first time in 12, 13, 14 years of their life, they sit down and plan how the next year — and the next five — should look. Not because a parent said so. Not because a teacher said so. Because they chose it.

"They set goals that are meaningful to them. And they understand why decisions matter — and how to make better ones."
— Atray Agrawal

What we actually teach

Two skills that do not go out of fashion

Decision-making

Why decisions compound. How to make better ones. Frameworks and mental models from physics — first principles, inversion, bottlenecks, leverage — applied to their own life, not a textbook problem.

Goal-setting

They write goals that are theirs. Then we turn those into a year map: what to do, what to ignore, and a review rhythm so the plan does not die in week two.

The 7 days

Daily 1-hour sessions. Live. No long lectures.

Seven days in a row, one hour each. I speak for part of it. Then we work. By the end they have an annual plan they can actually use.

Days 1–2

Inventory. Past decisions. Patterns. What they actually value when the options are limited.

Days 3–5

Frameworks. First principles, 80/20, bottlenecks, inversion. Then three major goals that survive a sceptical adult.

Days 6–7

The year map, one leverage habit, a review system, and a public commitment so it does not stay in a notebook.

They leave with

Not a certificate. A working system.

An Annual Plan

Written by them. Goals, constraints, and the next actions — not a vision board.

A living calendar

The year laid out so they can see where the work actually sits.

One leverage ritual

Small enough that they will do it. Identity-based, not willpower-based.

A review rhythm

Daily, weekly, monthly questions so the plan keeps moving after day 7.

Questions

Straight answers

What age is this for?

12 and up. Younger than that, the work does not stick the same way. Mixed cohorts are fine — the older students often pull the younger ones up.

My child already does well in school. Why this?

This is not tutoring. High-achievers are often excellent at following directions and weak at setting their own. That gap shows up later, when nobody is handing them a syllabus.

They don't know what they want.

That is a good reason to come, not a reason to wait. We use thought experiments to try on futures before they commit to one.

Is this the same as the Bootcamp or Fellowship?

No. Design Your Life is clarity. Future Founders Bootcamp is build-and-sell. The Fellowship is mentorship for a global competition. This is the first step if they do not yet have direction.

If they have never planned their own year, this is the place to start.
