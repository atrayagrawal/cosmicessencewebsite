Student Projects

What Teenagers Can Build When Given Real Agency.

When you treat high school students as capable thinkers rather than passive test-takers, they solve real problems. Here are authentic projects built by teenagers across our programs and partner immersions.

In partnership with Indian Women Impact (NGO)

The Global Scholarship Directory

High school students from non-elite backgrounds often miss out on transformative global scholarships simply because the information is scattered, confusing, and hard to find. This student cohort spent weeks researching, verifying, and indexing over 100 international funding opportunities into a clean public directory for other teenagers.

Student Growth

Students went from feeling like passive recipients to realizing they can build real tools that solve problems for others.

Download Project Document (PDF)
The Challenge

Global scholarships exist for youth, but navigating fragmented websites, foreign deadlines, and eligibility criteria is overwhelming for teenagers without private counselors.

How They Built It

Students learned internet research rigor, structured database fields (grade level, financial coverage, citizenship criteria), verified genuine deadlines, and published an accessible directory.

Growth & Capability

Public presentation confidence, data organizing skills, and the pride of publishing an asset that reached families in India and abroad.

Project Aether: Orbital Space Habitat

A team of high school students modeled a self-sustaining orbital human habitat. Guided by first-principles physics rather than memorized textbook formulas, they calculated rotational artificial gravity (1g), structural load distributions, radiation protection, and closed-loop life-support cycles.

Student Growth

Instead of preparing for standard school exams through memorization, the team learned how real engineers solve open-ended scientific problems.

Download NASA Project Dossier (PDF)
The Challenge

How can humans live long-term in space without bone/muscle loss from microgravity and radiation damage?

How They Built It

Calculated the exact radius and rotational speed needed to simulate Earth gravity without inducing nausea. Designed shielding materials, closed-loop hydroponics, and structural support trusses across a 40+ page engineering blueprint.

Growth & Capability

Students discovered that physics isn't just about passing tests—it's a tool to design things that don't exist yet.

NASA Space Settlement Contest Submission • Mentored by Atray Agrawal

HEXA NEURO: Asteroid Mining & Mars Base

Students tackled one of space travel's biggest hurdles: the prohibitive fuel and economic cost of launching raw materials from Earth. They modeled how to redirect a near-Earth asteroid (Asteroid 1992-BF), extract metals and water directly in orbit, and use them to support an orbital base near Mars.

Student Growth

Students learned orbital mechanics and systems thinking, realizing that complex problems become solvable when broken down into foundational physical principles.

Download NASA Project Dossier (PDF)
The Challenge

Deep-space human presence is economically impossible if every kilogram of water, metal, and structural material must be lifted out of Earth's gravity well.

How They Built It

Modeled orbital trajectories for asteroid redirection, estimated resource extraction economics, and engineered closed-loop life-support systems.

Growth & Capability

Cross-grade collaboration, defending technical ideas under scientific critique, and writing publication-grade mathematical documentation.

Summer Intensive • NGO & IB Student Cohorts

Designing Your Life: Self-Direction Journals

Teenagers today face intense exam pressure, often without any space to reflect on what they value or how they want to build their future. In this intensive, students conducted daily energy audits, dismantled passive habits, and constructed personalized 3-year roadmaps.

Student Growth

Students replaced academic anxiety and compliance with self-directed agency, building physical life journals and presenting their goals to peers.

The Challenge

Students are trained to score marks on tests, but often feel completely lost or anxious when asked what they actually want to do with their lives.

How They Built It

Interactive workshop sprints covering energy tracking, cognitive reframing, personal value audits, and peer presentation circles.

Growth & Capability

Developed public defense skills, self-awareness, and the ability to make deliberate decisions about their own education and time.
